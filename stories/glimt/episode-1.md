# Glimt, episode 1: Keep Going

English version 1, 2026-10-23, translated from the Swedish draft 7 (`episod-1.md`, app version 38). Henric decided on 23/10 that Glimt goes out in English. Five changes he asked for are in this version, and the pauses are marked from his walk the same morning (what changed is at the end). Recorded in app version 41 (27/10, Paula as Vega), and again in version 45 (29/10) with Jessica as Vega, Henric's choice, from the text of Manus version 8. Version 46 (29/10) adds her "Now." after each question she asks for a stop, since she promises it (Manus version 9); the stop is counted from her Now. Version 51 (29/10, Manus version 11, Henric's two notes): the star she wants is a wish, not a task, so nothing asks the walker to go out at night; and she says only what she sees of the walker's world, so the two lines about the loop being a copy are struck and kept as a thread for a later episode (Planted, at the end). Only the changed lines are recorded again. Version 55 (29/10, Manus version 13): "Go however you like. If you have to stop, stop. Your road comes before mine." is struck from scene 1, for it broke the illusion (Henric); the start screen says it instead, outside the story. Cut out of the take, nothing recorded.

**What the episode has to do:** Vega and the walker meet. The walker should understand three things without anyone explaining them: she sees glimpses of your world but never hears you, she can only be heard while you move, and your steps hide her.

**The arc:** she talks out into thin air, gets an answer, and uses her first freedom on something small. She stands still. She touches a tree. She sits down on the ground and sees, for the first time, a bit of your sky.

**Style:** close and spoken, one person on a bad phone line. Realism, with poetry used sparingly. She teaches the rules by getting them wrong herself, and the feeling sits in what breaks, not in descriptions. She says "here" and "where you are", never what year it is and never where "here" is. She describes only what she can see of your world: the light, the weather, how you move and what the map has near you. Never people, cars or things.

**The English:** spoken English of a young working woman. Not literary, not sitcom. Short lines that sound right read aloud. Contractions where people use them. No slang that pins her to one country, so the accent is the voice's choice.

**Target length:** about ten and a half minutes once the scenes follow each other instead of waiting for the clock (see Pauses at the end). About seven minutes of her voice, a minute and a half of counting, waiting and her side, and a good minute of credits. Counted on Henric's way on 23/10: about 10:50 at the Swedish voice's pace in version 38, about 9:45 at 150 words a minute; 9:40 to 11:30 across the answers, more for a walker who meets red lights. Nothing in the episode needs speed, so it works as well running as walking.

**The logic, so the scenes hold together:**
- Vega sees glimpses of the walker's world: the movement (walking, running, standing), the light, the weather and what the map has near the walker. Blurred, at the edge, never people or objects. The walker is only a shape at the edge of Vega's world, never a face. No sound crosses over. So she knows when the walker moves and stands, but never hears what the walker says. The walker answers her only by stopping.
- The workers wear an earpiece that counts their steps. While the walker moves, it counts the walker's steps instead of hers, and the Company sees a worker on her round, whatever she does. The earpiece doesn't hear what she says either, as long as the walker moves. She never names the earpiece: she says "they know".
- When the walker stands still, it counts her own steps. If she takes them where she isn't allowed, the lamp comes on.
- If she stands still too, there are no steps to count. That holds for half a minute, then the earpiece asks what she is doing. So a short stop is harmless while she walks her round as usual (scenes 0 and 1, and scene 2 until she steps off the gravel) and while she sits still (scene 3), but dangerous when she moves where she isn't allowed to be (scene 2 from the gravel, and the start of scene 4).
- Her voice always goes muffled when the walker stands still. What you hear instead is her side: the hum of the loop, a tone, a flat voice.
- Her own steps are never heard (Henric 21/10). Her side has the hum, the lamp's tone, the bell, the hand on the tree and, in scene 4, the shift's steps far away.
- Reserve in every scene: if the walker stands still for more than half a minute while she stands still too, the earpiece asks "Vega. You are stationary. Do you need assistance?". She answers "No, thank you. I'm resting." and the story goes on when the walker moves.

**Voices:** Vega. The earpiece, a couple of lines: in English a man's voice, very soft, steady and even, calm and polite, close to the microphone, with no feeling in it (Henric 23/10: something like HAL 9000, not a copy of it). Not cold and robotic, and not thin.

Pauses are marked where Henric's walk on 23/10 had them too long or too short, as "(pause about N s; was M s on 23/10)". The app follows them since version 41 (27/10), except the breath before "I'm standing still.": about 1.1 s, so the half minute still holds.

## Changed in the English version (23/10)

Henric's five changes:

1. **Shape, not the Walker.** The name comes from what she sees, not from the walking, so it fits a runner as well as a walker. She has seen the shape at the edge of her world many times; tonight is the first time it answers. Planted three times before it becomes a name: in the new opening ("There it is again. Right at the edge.", quiet under the riser), in her first words to you ("A shape at the edge"), and in scene 3 ("you're still just a shape"). Paid off in the name scene in scene 4, in all three branches. Altair is not mentioned.
2. **More of her talking to herself.** A new opening of three short lines to herself before she calls out: she sees the shape again, talks herself out of it, and does it anyway. Her voice enters at 70 % of the riser, so the three lines make the start longer: "Hello?" comes about 23 s in instead of 14. Worth it for the eavesdropping; if it is too long, cut the middle line ("It's the lights...") and "Hello?" comes about 18 s in. Two more short ones: "Three metres. It's only three metres." before she steps off the gravel, and "Up. Up, up." when the bell rings. All five are marked (to herself).
3. **"You hear me" once more.** After "I see you. / And you hear me." she says it again, almost to herself, as the song begins. This is the end of the opening, before the credits: the only place "I see you, and you hear me" stands.
4. **The pauses.** Marked in the text where they should be shorter or longer, measured in the walk on 23/10 (table below).
5. **The Code under her last words.** The song starts faint under her last line before the credits and grows to full volume when she has finished, in both branches.

Translation choices:

- **"Go" is the signal word**, never "walk": she says "go" to a runner too. The go lines end on the word "go", as in the Swedish draft 7.
- **The title** is "Keep Going", her own refrain ("Keep going", "Just keep going"), and it fits a runner. The Swedish "Det är när du går" said walking.
- **The earpiece is never named** in her lines; she says "they", "they know", "it's been logged". In the stage directions it is the earpiece.
- **"Du är steg, inte öron"** stays as "You're steps, not ears": odd in English, but it is her and it comes back in "Then you're steps" in scene 4.
- **Scene 2 starts with the idea itself.** "Jag har tänkt på en sak" came right after "en dum idé" and said the same thing twice once the pause is short, so the English goes straight to "Here's the thing."
- Small lines rewritten for English rhythm, same meaning: "You heard me. I didn't think I'd get this far." "The lamp caught one out of three." "You move, I'm hidden." "I can tell, you're going straight now" (was "you walk straighter", which a runner doesn't).

After the director's and the dramaturg's reviews (23/10, reports in Demi's `data/glimt-granska/2026-10-23-*.md`):

- Every go line ends on "go": "I can't see you when you stand still. Go." (was "...when you stop", the wrong order last), and both pleas at the tree.
- "You'd already disappeared when I asked" (was "gone", which reads as "walked off"), and "There, I've lost you. Now go." (was "You're gone", too close to "go").
- "comes around the corner" (was "round the bend", British for going mad).
- "Okay" kept once in the opening and once in scene 4; the other four replaced.
- Words that tied her to Britain before the voice is chosen: "this loop", "pretty", "lots of times", "I've been going on about".
- The name: "So you're Shape. That's what I've been calling you in my head. (pause) It'll do."
- "But here you are anyway" (was "walking here", which leaves out a runner).
- The shape is planted where it is heard (see change 1), and "stupid" no longer comes twice across the short join into scene 2.
