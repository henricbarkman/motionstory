# Glimt, episode 3: The Errand

English draft 1, 2026-10-31, written in Manus after episode 2 (v59) and the core loop of 30/10. Reviewed the same night by the dramaturg and the director (generalassistant data/glimt-granska/2026-10-31-*), their findings worked in before it went into Manus. Not recorded: Henric reads it first, and the two new voice modes need samples.

**What the episode has to do:** Vega carries the book to the hatch by the charging station, and she can only get there on Shape's steps. Afterwards Shape should know three new things without anyone explaining them: in the corridors of the works she is bound to Shape's steps, so Shape going faster is her going faster; what the Company watches is the steps, not her, and they have to look like the room, so among the machines that stand charging a stop is what hides her; and the words the workers use mean more than they sound like.

**The arc:** the book didn't reach the hatch and slept under her mattress, or she is about to try right now, straight after episode 2. Through the doors that count steps, into the hall where the machines charge, past the shift manager, and when the night bell goes she runs for the first time since she was little, or the hatch shuts in front of her and someone on the other side knocks. On the way back the earpiece greets her for the first time, and she knows what it means.

**Style:** as episodes 1 and 2. Close and spoken, one person on a bad phone line, dry when it's at its worst. She describes only what the phone has read: how Shape moves, stops and knocks, and how fast. Nothing about the place where Shape is in this episode. The people on her side never speak in their own voices: she tells Shape what they say. What she says to herself is marked (to herself), recorded with [to herself]: her normal voice, a little lower. Her own steps are never heard (Henric 21/10): when she runs, you hear her breath.

**The English:** as episodes 1 and 2, in Jessica's American.

**Liv's words:** eight passages from Liv's Here are in Vega's mouth, word for word, with only the spelling fixed, cut only at a full stop. Nothing that says where Vega is: in two of them one sentence is left out, the one with "before launch" (Here 11) and the one with "vacuum" (Here 16). They are listed at the end. The longer ones are recorded a sentence at a time, each with its tag, so the aside doesn't drift back to her normal voice halfway.

**Length:** about eight to nine minutes on one walk at episode 1's pace: about 900 words of hers on one path, the three doors, the manager passing, forty seconds of bell. A little less when the manager sees her (no run).

**Walking or running:** scene 0 goes as well running. From the corridors in scene 1 she asks a runner to walk, because in there Shape running is her running, and nobody runs in there; the phone keeps it as [ran before the doors] if Shape was running when scene 1 began, otherwise [walked before the doors]. The knock question in scene 1 is skipped for anyone running then. When the night bell goes in scene 3 she lets a runner run again. On the way back walking again, and on the loop it's free.

**The logic, beyond episode 2's:**

- What the Company watches is the steps, not her. While Shape moves, the steps they count are Shape's. A manager doesn't look at people, he looks at his wrist, and on his wrist she is walking as long as Shape is. The steps have to look like the room: in the corridors everyone walks, behind the presses everyone walks in time, in the charging hall everything stands still.
- Bound in the corridors. Every door between the presses and the charging hall clocks her, and from one door to the next they count steps: Shape's steps. If she got from door to door faster than those steps could carry her, it would show. So in the corridors she is exactly as fast as Shape, and the last stretch, from the third door to the hatch, counts too. If Shape stops in a corridor, her steps stop, in a corridor where nobody stands still, and the lamp comes on, as mood only, as in episode 2. (Henric's choice, see Your choices.)
- Among the machines it's the other way round. In the charging hall everything stands still, plugged into the wall, so when someone looks, walking is what shows. There she asks Shape to freeze (Frys), and the stop she asks for hides her. A stop she didn't ask for is harmless there.
- The hatch by the charging station is where the day shift passes things to the night shift. It is open only while the night bell rings, about forty seconds. When the bell goes, the night shift comes in and the machines roll out, so for those forty seconds everything in the hall moves, and even a run doesn't show. But from the third door to the hatch she has only Shape's steps, and walking that takes about a minute. So when the bell goes she needs Shape faster.
- Fast, once. Faster as in episode 2's app: Shape's step rate above what it was before her "Now fast", for about twelve seconds within the forty the bell rings. The step rate before is measured over the half minute before, leaving out the first ten seconds after the freeze, so getting going again doesn't count. Light consequences only, since Fortare is Delvis: the book gets through either way. Faster: she reaches the hatch in time and puts it in herself. Not faster: the hatch shuts in front of her, it opens again after two knocks from the other side, Shape is asked to answer with two knocks, and a hand takes the book.
- The manager. If Shape doesn't freeze when she asks, or walks again too early, the manager sees the only steps in the hall that are walking. He watches her, and when the bell goes she can't run while he does: she walks, and is late to the hatch, the same branch as not faster. By then he has turned to the night shift. The run is missed, not the book. Frys holds, so this may decide more than a knock may. Whether being seen means anything later is not decided: the phone only remembers it.
- Knocks as in episode 2: two quick knocks within about ten seconds of her "Now", light consequences only. Knocks are read standing too, and a stop inside those ten seconds doesn't turn the lamp on.
- Talking as in episode 2: what counts her steps can't hear her while Shape moves, but people near her can. When the manager is close she whispers or says nothing.

**Reserve in every scene:** if both stand still longer than half a minute, the earpiece asks. On the loop she answers with episode 1's lines; in the works she answers "(muffled, lightly) Shoelace."

## Your choices

Three things in this episode are yours to decide. Each has the draft's proposal, written in above, and one alternative.

- The lamp. Proposal: as in episode 2, mood only. A stop in a corridor gives the tone, the earpiece and her muffled voice, the whole branch only the first time, and nothing carries on. New in this episode: in the charging hall it is the other way round, and a stop is what hides her. Alternative: the lamp costs something small inside the episode, a door that won't open and a longer way round, about half a minute more.
- Faster. Proposal: once, when the night bell goes. Faster: she gets there in time and puts the book in herself. Not faster: the hatch shuts, someone knocks from the other side, Shape knocks back, and a hand takes the book. It gets through either way. Alternative: not faster means the book stays with her another night, which would only be fair once Fortare holds out on the street.
- What binds her in the works. Proposal: the doors clock her and count Shape's steps from door to door, and on to the hatch, so she can never be faster than Shape there; elsewhere she moves freely. Alternative: the Company locks her body in the works with something it has put in her (the world book's variant A), so only Shape's steps move her. That one also breaks "I walked home with the shift" and the dropped glove, which need a little rewriting.
